The wavelet ℓ¹ norm is a powerful non-Gaussian summary statistic for
weak-lensing maps. It collapses, however, the moment you apply the BNT
(Bernardeau–Nishimichi–Taruya) transform — even with cross-channel maps.
The CNN, trained on the same maps, does not.

Three explainers walk through why that happens and where the information
goes. Scroll through each one; press **R** to replay the active animation.

  <Beat n={1}>
    Four maps in channel-space → a fixed cloud of pixels. A per-channel
    ℓ¹ norm is the cloud's shadow on one axis.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={2}>
    BNT shears the measuring axes off the cloud's long direction. The
    new shadows go blank. **The cloud itself never moves.**
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={3}>
    The cosmology lives in the cloud's shape, not in any single-map
    shadow. In this frame, marginal histograms can't see it.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={4}>
    The CNN mixes channels in its first layer and draws its own axis
    back along the cloud. It's basis-robust, not "smarter."
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={5}>
    Whitening rotates to a different clean frame — per-map shadows are
    rich again. Nothing was lost; the collapse was about the choice
    of basis.
  </Beat>
</RevealExplainer>

## 2. Signal and noise under BNT — the mechanism engine

> **Editorial placeholder.** The recovery-ladder narrative
> (auto → +cross → CNN → whiten). Andreas refines.

<RevealExplainer
  module="bnt_explainer"
  attach="BNTExplainer"
  kind="mechanism"
  acts={5}
  sectionClassName="bnt-slide"
  sectionContent={<BNTMechanismSection />}
>
  <Beat n={1}>
    Four tomographic maps share a common signal under noise. The auto
    ℓ¹ tally reads the signal directly.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={2}>
    BNT differences the maps. The common signal collapses into a
    shallow map; what remains is thin lens-redshift slices with
    amplified, correlated noise.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={3}>
    Per-map auto-ℓ¹ in the new basis sees mostly the amplified noise.
    The recovery ladder shows the FoM falling.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={4}>
    Adding cross-maps recovers some of the signal: the cross terms
    encode the cloud's joint structure. The CNN does even better
    because it can mix freely.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={5}>
    Whitening makes the noise covariance back into identity. The
    signal that BNT scrambled is recoverable in this rotated frame.
  </Beat>
</RevealExplainer>

## 3. The 2-point fingerprint — the twopoint engine

> **Editorial placeholder.** What share of the BNT collapse is
> genuinely two-point structure vs irreducibly joint. Andreas refines.

<RevealExplainer
  module="bnt_explainer"
  attach="BNTExplainer"
  kind="twopoint"
  acts={5}
  sectionClassName="bnt-slide"
  sectionContent={<BNTTwoPointSection />}
>
  <Beat n={1}>
    The auto+cross spectrum matrix C of the four maps. The diagonal is
    auto-power; the off-diagonals are cross-power.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={2}>
    BNT acts on C by congruence: B C Bᵀ. The whole matrix transforms
    invertibly — no information is lost at the two-point level.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={3}>
    Keeping only the diagonal (auto-only ℓ¹) throws away the
    off-diagonal information. That projection is not invertible.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={4}>
    The collapse measured at the two-point level matches the
    auto-only-vs-full gap. Much of the ℓ¹-norm collapse is
    two-point-recoverable.
  </Beat>
  <Beat n={5}>
    But there's a residue: the part of the higher-order ℓ¹ structure
    that is genuinely joint and irreducibly multi-map. The CNN's
    learned mixing captures it; auto-only summaries can't.
  </Beat>
</RevealExplainer>
